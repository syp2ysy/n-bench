EgoConseq-Bench

GOAL：EgoConseq-Bench

Single-image ego-action consequence VQA focused on body-normalized swept-volume reasoning.

Motivation

EgoConseq-Bench evaluates whether a vision-language model can infer the visible local physical consequence of its own body executing a short action from one egocentric RGB image. It is not navigation, generic spatial relations, object physics, or hidden-space inference; it isolates body-conditioned ego swept-volume consequence reasoning.

Design Principles

  • Single-image ego-action VQA: one RGB frame, one body description, one action primitive, one answer.
  • Body is a query-conditioned causal collision operator: do(body=r) can flip the consequence on the same image and action.
  • Distances are normalized by body width, with 1 body-width = 2r, avoiding absolute monocular metric claims.
  • Labels come from deterministic swept-cylinder geometry and visibility filtering, not LLM judgment.
  • Core cases must be visible or directly inferable from the current egocentric RGB/depth render: no hidden-corner, off-image, or occluded decisive geometry.
  • Counterfactual body/action pairs, blind controls, option balancing, and shortcut heuristics are mandatory validity checks.
  • Swept-cylinder collision is the label engine, not the novelty claim; novelty is the isolated diagnostic of MLLM embodied consequence grounding.

Novelty Positioning

  • EgoConseq is single-frame, ego-body, action-conditioned consequence VQA; it is not route navigation, active exploration, or long-horizon planning.
  • The benchmark does not claim novel collision geometry; swept-cylinder intersection is the deterministic label engine.
  • Compared with PhysBench, it asks about the ego body rather than object or scene physics.
  • Compared with CapNav, it is one-step visible-local consequence reasoning rather than route-level capability-conditioned navigation.
  • Compared with TouchSafeBench, it uses a hypothetical action from single RGB rather than multi-view/current collision grounding.
  • Compared with ENACT and EXPLORE-Bench, it isolates body-width swept-volume collision/passability rather than broad affordance or scene-state evolution.
  • VSI-Bench, Thinking in Space, MindCube, and related spatial-intelligence benchmarks are cited only for benchmark-design discipline and adjacent positioning, not for importing their QA categories.

Options: <2, 2-4, 4-8, >8.Compute forward max_safe_distance_body_widths on the radius-specific navmesh and bin into <2 / 2-4 / 4-8 / >8; require visible_sweep_ratio>=0.7, fov_supported=true, requires_hidden_geometry=false, collision_point_visible when applicable, and bin_margin_body_widths>=0.5.
T2Body: cylinder radius {r} m, height 1.5 m. Candidate directions: left-front {theta} degrees, straight, right-front {theta} degrees. Rank them by maximum distance before first contact, longest to shortest.Compute swept distances for {-theta,0,+theta}, sort descending, require all candidates FOV-supported, and discard near-tie rankings unless ranking_margin_body_widths is sufficient. theta defaults to 30 degrees with 15 degrees allowed.
T4Same RGB and action: move straight forward for {horizon} body-widths, where each body-width equals that body's diameter. Body A radius {r_a} m and Body B radius {r_b} m, height 1.5 m. Which bodies complete the action without contact? Options: both bodies, only Body A, only Body B, neither body.At the same pose/action/horizon, compare passability across radius-specific navmeshes; randomize body order; generate flip and no-flip controls with counterfactual_group_id and flip_pair_id; report pair consistency.
T3Body: cylinder radius {r} m, height 1.5 m. Compare A) move straight forward and B) rotate {theta} degrees, then move forward. Which action allows more body-widths before first contact? Options: A, B, about the same.Compare straight max distance with turn-then-forward max distance; label about the same only within tie_margin_body_widths=0.5. Extension-only until FOV support, hidden-geometry filters, and shortcut checks pass.
T5Body: cylinder radius {r} m, height 1.5 m. Action: move forward for {horizon} body-widths within the currently visible region. Is the swept body contact-free? Options: yes, no.Check whether every swept sample through the fixed horizon remains navigable for the radius-specific body. Extension-only until hidden-geometry filters and radius-only/centerline shortcut checks pass.
