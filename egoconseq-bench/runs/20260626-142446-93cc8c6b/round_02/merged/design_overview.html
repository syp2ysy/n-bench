EgoConseq-Bench

GOAL：EgoConseq-Bench

Single-image ego-action consequence VQA focused on body-normalized swept-volume reasoning.

Motivation

EgoConseq-Bench evaluates whether a vision-language model can infer the visible local physical consequence of its own body executing a short action from one egocentric RGB image. The target is not navigation, object physics, or generic spatial relations; it is body-conditioned ego swept-volume consequence reasoning.

Design Principles

  • Single-image ego-action VQA: one RGB frame, one body description, one action primitive, one answer.
  • Body is a query-conditioned causal collision operator: do(body=r) can flip the consequence on the same image.
  • Distances are body-width normalized, with 1 body-width = 2r, avoiding absolute metric-distance claims from monocular RGB.
  • Labels come from deterministic swept-cylinder geometry and visibility filtering, not LLM judgment.
  • Core cases must be visible or directly inferable: no hidden-corner, off-image, or occluded decisive geometry.
  • Counterfactual body/action pairs, blind controls, option balancing, and shortcut heuristics are mandatory validity checks.
  • Swept-cylinder collision is the label engine, not the novelty claim; the novelty is the isolated diagnostic of MLLM embodied consequence grounding.

Novelty Positioning

  • EgoConseq is single-frame, ego-body, action-conditioned consequence VQA; it is not route navigation, active exploration, or long-horizon planning.
  • The benchmark does not claim novel collision geometry; swept-cylinder intersection is the deterministic label engine.
  • Compared with PhysBench, it asks about the ego body rather than object or scene physics.
  • Compared with CapNav, it is one-step visible-local consequence reasoning rather than route-level capability-conditioned navigation.
  • Compared with TouchSafeBench, it uses a hypothetical action from single RGB rather than multi-view/current collision grounding.
  • Compared with ENACT and EXPLORE-Bench, it isolates body-width swept-volume collision/passability rather than broad affordance or scene-state evolution.
  • VSI-Bench, Thinking in Space, MindCube, and ESI-Bench are cited only for benchmark-design discipline and adjacent positioning, not for importing their QA categories.

T1Body: cylinder radius {r} m, height 1.5 m. Action: move straight forward. How many body-widths can it move before first contact? Options: <2, 2-4, 4-8, >8.Compute forward max_safe_distance_body_widths on the radius-specific navmesh and bin into <2 / 2-4 / 4-8 / >8; require bin_margin_body_widths >= 0.5.
T2Body: cylinder radius {r} m, height 1.5 m. Candidate directions: left-front {theta} degrees, straight, right-front {theta} degrees. Rank them by maximum distance before first contact, longest to shortest.Compute swept distances for {-theta,0,+theta}, sort descending, and require visible evidence plus a ranking margin sufficient to avoid ties; theta defaults to 30 degrees with 15 degrees allowed.
T4Same RGB and action: move straight forward for {horizon} body-widths. Compare Body A radius {r_a} m and Body B radius {r_b} m, height 1.5 m. Which bodies complete the action without contact? Options use both / only smaller body / neither, unless body order is randomized.At the same pose/action/horizon, compare passability across radius-specific navmeshes; produce flip and no-flip controls with counterfactual_group_id and flip_pair_id.
T3Body: cylinder radius {r} m, height 1.5 m. Compare A) move straight forward and B) rotate {theta} degrees, then move forward. Which action allows more body-widths before first contact? Options: A, B, about the same.Compare straight max distance with turn-then-forward max distance; label about the same only within a fixed tie margin of 0.5 body-width. Extension-only until FOV support is verified.
T5Body: cylinder radius {r} m, height 1.5 m. Action: move forward for {horizon} body-widths within the currently visible region. Is the swept body contact-free? Options: yes, no.Check whether every swept sample through the fixed horizon remains navigable for the radius-specific body. Extension-only until hidden-geometry and shortcut risks are audited.
